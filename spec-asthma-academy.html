Welcome to SPEC Asthma Academy

Start Here — Who This Course Is For

Asthma affects an estimated 300 million people worldwide and remains a leading cause of preventable pediatric hospitalization. Despite evidence-based guidelines, diagnosis is frequently delayed, inhaler technique is routinely unchecked, and SABA-only treatment — now recognized as inadequate — remains widespread. This course is built for everyone who cares for children with asthma and wants to do it better.

Curriculum Structure

Core Modules

Ten structured modules covering the full spectrum of pediatric asthma — updated throughout for GINA 2026. Each module includes learning objectives, resident pearls, family teaching points, self-assessment questions, and a case vignette. GINA 2026 changes are flagged with a ★ 2026 badge in the relevant modules.

01
Asthma Foundations and Why It Matters
Pathophysiology, global burden, variable airflow limitation, the distinction between symptom relief and anti-inflammatory therapy
Foundations
02
Diagnosing Asthma — Children 6 Years and Older
Symptom patterns, spirometry, bronchodilator response, differential diagnosis, diagnostic workflow per GINA 2026 and NAEPP 2020. 2026 update: evidence on bronchodilator responsiveness criteria — the 2022 >10% predicted criterion may underdiagnose asthma, particularly in younger males; the 2005 ERS/ATS criterion (≥12% and ≥200 mL) is retained.
Diagnosis ★ 2026
03
Diagnosing Asthma — Children Younger Than 6 Years
Recurrent wheeze phenotypes, API and mAPI tools, therapeutic trial approach, ERS 2024 guidance, red flags, referral triggers. Diagnosis criteria revised in GINA 2025 remain current in GINA 2026.
Preschool — Complex
04
Assessing Asthma Control, Risk, and Follow-Up
Symptom control vs. future risk, GINA 2026 control assessment, exacerbation history, lung function, adherence, follow-up structure. 2026 additions: Chronic Airways Assessment Test (CAAT) for comprehensive adult health-status evaluation; Pediatric Asthma Impairment and Risk Questionnaire (Peds-AIRQ) for children 5–11 years — a composite score linking symptoms, reliever use, and exacerbation history.
Assessment ★ 2026
05
Pediatric Asthma Medications and Step Therapy
ICS as cornerstone, SABA overuse as warning sign, GINA Track 1 (preferred — ICS-formoterol AIR/MART, Steps 1–4) vs. Track 2 (alternative — ICS-SABA AIR, now extended to Step 1 per GINA 2026/BATURA study); CARE study evidence for as-needed ICS-formoterol in children 6–11 years at Steps 1–2; NAEPP vs. GINA 2026 reconciliation; dose-prompt safety language replacing "maximum dose" wording.
Pharmacology ★ 2026
06
Inhalers, Spacers, Masks, and Device Teaching
pMDI/HFA, DPI, RediHaler, Respimat, Diskus, Flexhaler, Ellipta, nebulizers — device selection, spacer use, demonstration + return demonstration. 2026 updates: all pMDIs formulated as suspensions (including salbutamol, budesonide-formoterol, and fluticasone-salmeterol) must be shaken immediately before each actuation — even a 30-second delay causes significant dose variation. Single-breath-with-breath-hold technique is preferred for routine pMDI + spacer use; tidal breathing is reserved for preschoolers and acute exacerbations.
Device Skills ★ 2026
07
Acute Asthma Exacerbations and Emergency Management
Four new standardized GINA 2026 flowcharts for primary care and ED (adults/adolescents/children 6–11 years; and children ≤5 years separately). Severity recognition using validated PRAM score (strongly recommended for patients <18 years). Revised oxygen targets: supplement only when SpO₂ <92%; upper target 95% for adults, adolescents, and children 6–11 years. Conservative SABA dosing (caution re: lactic acidosis and over-treatment). ICS-formoterol now an option for mild exacerbations in primary care or ED (alternative to SABA) — enables earlier introduction of anti-inflammatory reliever and facilitates MART initiation before discharge. OCS stewardship. Discharge requirements and follow-up timing.
Emergency ★ 2026
08
Action Plans, Discharge Teaching, and Self-Management
Written asthma action plans, green/yellow/red zone framework, family communication language, discharge checklist, school considerations. 2026 update: action plan wording revised — "seek care if you need more than X inhalations in 24 hours" replaces prior "maximum dose" language, for safety. OCS stewardship is a stated priority in GINA 2026: any exacerbation requiring OCS is a red flag for treatment review.
Self-Management ★ 2026
09
Difficult-to-Treat and Severe Asthma
Definitions, modifiable factors before labeling "severe," comorbidities, phenotype/endotype basics. 2026 biologic updates: depemokimab (long-acting anti-IL5, SC every 26 weeks, approved from age 12 for severe eosinophilic asthma) and first biosimilar anti-IgE (omalizumab-igec, same indications as omalizumab) now included. Updated biologic-selection decision tree with clearer guidance on predictors of response, relevant comorbidities, dosing frequency, and patient preference. Step 5 triple therapy: budesonide-formoterol-glycopyrronium (ICS-LABA-LAMA) added as option. Referral thresholds.
Advanced ★ 2026

💨 MDI with Spacer (Valved Holding Chamber) Core
Demonstrates the pressurized metered-dose inhaler used with a spacer device — the preferred delivery method for ICS in children using a pMDI. Covers priming, shaking, timing, and breath-hold.
⚡ GINA 2026 Update: All suspension pMDIs (including salbutamol, budesonide-formoterol, fluticasone-salmeterol) must be shaken immediately before each actuation — a delay of even 30 seconds can cause significant dose variation. The single-breath-with-breath-hold technique is preferred for routine use; tidal breathing is used for preschoolers and during acute exacerbations.

🔵 RediHaler (Breath-Actuated MDI) Device-Specific
The RediHaler is a breath-actuated device that releases medication automatically upon inhalation, eliminating the coordination challenge of a standard pMDI. Spacer is not used. Technique differs significantly from a standard MDI.

🌬️ Respimat (Soft Mist Inhaler) Device-Specific
The Respimat produces a slow-moving aerosol cloud (soft mist), requiring less inspiratory effort than DPIs and allowing more time for inhalation than standard MDIs. Used for tiotropium in pediatric asthma and for some combination inhalers.

For Residents
Teaching with Videos
  • Use the video before or after live technique teaching as preparation or reinforcement
  • Pause at key steps and ask the learner or family to identify what was just demonstrated
  • Ask: "What common mistakes do you think people make with this device?"
  • 2026 teaching point: Explicitly ask and demonstrate — "When do you shake this inhaler?" Correct answer: immediately before each actuation, every time, without exception
  • Perform teach-back immediately after — ask the patient or family to demonstrate with the actual device
  • Document: "Inhaler technique reviewed with demonstration and return demonstration; supplemental device video from Children's Hospital Colorado provided."
For Families
Watch at Home, Then Practice
  • Watch the video for your child's specific device at home — not just once, but before each refill season
  • Practice with the inhaler in your hands while watching
  • Important (2026 update): For spray-type inhalers (not the powder kind), shake the inhaler right before every single puff — even if you only set it down for a moment
  • Bring the inhaler and spacer to every follow-up visit so your clinician can check your technique
  • Technique drifts over time — even experienced users benefit from a recheck every 6–12 months
  • If the video and your inhaler look different, call your pharmacy or provider — confirm you have the right device

iOS · Android · Sensor-based
A sensor-connected platform that attaches to inhalers (SABA and controller) and tracks medication use in real time. Provides adherence data, trigger mapping, and personalized feedback. Research has shown improvement in controller adherence and reduction in SABA use with this platform.
AdherenceSensor-ConnectedPatient/Clinician

Asthma Tracker (Asthma MD)
iOS · Android
A symptom and peak flow diary with trigger logging, medication reminders, and physician-shareable reports. Useful for families monitoring day-to-day control and documenting patterns between clinical visits.

AirVisual (IQAir)
iOS · Android · Free
Real-time outdoor air quality monitoring using the Air Quality Index (AQI) and particulate matter data. Particularly relevant for families in West Texas and Eastern New Mexico where dust storms, wildfire smoke, and seasonal pollen events are common asthma triggers.

iOS · Android · Free

For Faculty & Preceptors

Teaching This Curriculum

Practical guidance for using SPEC Asthma Academy in clinic-based teaching, noon conference, and inpatient rounds — including observation checklists and direct assessment tools. Updated for GINA 2026.

Clinic Teaching
How to Use This in Ambulatory Settings
  • Assign one module per week of ambulatory rotation — learners review independently, debrief at pre-clinic
  • Use the Quick Reference checklists as structured observation tools during a patient encounter
  • Ask residents to perform a live inhaler technique check with every asthma patient — observe and score; specifically test the 2026 shaking rule for suspension pMDIs
  • Use Module 3 (preschool diagnosis) for any child under 6 presenting with recurrent wheeze — discuss uncertainty openly
  • After any ED visit or hospitalization, review the updated 2026 discharge checklist together — and flag any OCS use as a mandatory trigger for treatment review
Direct Observation & Assessment
Mini-CEX & OSCE Ideas
  • Inhaler Teaching OSCE: observe resident teaching a standardized patient to use a pMDI + spacer — score technique (including immediate shaking), teach-back, and documentation
  • Discharge Counseling OSCE: resident counsels a family after an ED visit — action plan with correct 2026 seek-care language, return precautions, follow-up
  • Spirometry Interpretation Station: provide a pre/post-bronchodilator spirometry — ask resident to interpret using the retained 2005 ERS/ATS BDR criterion (GINA 2026)
  • Preschool Diagnosis Station: present a 3-year-old with recurrent wheeze — resident must develop and explain a diagnostic approach
  • Difficult Asthma Station: present an "uncontrolled" child — resident must identify modifiable factors before escalating treatment and can describe depemokimab/biosimilar omalizumab as emerging biologic options per GINA 2026
Noon Conference & Inpatient Rounds
Short Teaching Formats
  • 5-Minute Pearl: "Why SABA-only treatment is no longer acceptable" — use Module 1 opening argument
  • 10-Minute Device Demo: bring three devices (MDI, spacer, DPI) — live demonstration with audience participation; include the 2026 shaking demonstration
  • Morning Report Case: use one of the 6 Module 10 case vignettes — structured discussion with GINA 2026 learning points
  • Grand Rounds Topic: "GINA 2026 — New Acute Flowcharts, Revised Oxygen Targets, ICS-SABA AIR at Step 1, CARE & BATURA Evidence, and Expanded Biologics: What Changes at the Bedside?"
Assessing Resident Performance
What Good Looks Like
  • A well-prepared resident diagnoses asthma using objective criteria, not just a label from a prior chart — and applies the retained 2005 ERS/ATS BDR criterion per GINA 2026
  • Demonstrates inhaler technique accurately themselves before teaching a family — including immediate pre-actuation shaking for all suspension pMDIs
  • Understands that "poor control" requires assessment of technique and adherence before escalation
  • Provides a written asthma action plan at every eligible encounter — using 2026 updated seek-care language, not max-dose language
  • Recognizes SABA overuse as a warning sign requiring same-day reassessment of controller therapy; identifies OCS use as a flag for mandatory treatment review

Tier 1b — Key Trials Newly Cited in GINA 2026
  • 5.CARE study (referenced in GINA 2026 as evidence base for as-needed ICS-formoterol in children 6–11 years at Steps 1–2): In children 5–15 years previously using SABA alone, low-dose budesonide-formoterol used as-needed for symptom relief reduced moderate-to-severe exacerbations by approximately half compared with SABA alone, without a difference in growth velocity. [ICS-formoterol formulation used in this study has been discontinued — see GINA 2026 Box 4-8B for the suggested alternative.] Reference details: cited as reference 20 in GINA 2026; consult GINA 2026 full report at ginasthma.org for complete citation.
  • 6.BATURA study (referenced in GINA 2026 as evidence base for Track 2 ICS-SABA AIR at Step 1): Among patients with poorly controlled asthma, as-needed ICS-SABA used either alone or as the reliever in patients taking daily ICS or LTRA reduced severe exacerbations by approximately half compared with SABA-only reliever. This evidence supports the 2026 addition of ICS-SABA combination AIR across all steps of Track 2, including Step 1. Reference details: cited as reference 21 in GINA 2026; consult GINA 2026 full report at ginasthma.org for complete citation.
  • 7.Bronchodilator responsiveness criterion evidence (referenced in GINA 2026): Analysis of large datasets showed that among adults with a positive BDR test by the 2005 ERS/ATS criterion (increase in FEV1 or FVC of ≥12% and ≥200 mL from baseline), a significant proportion tested negative under the 2022 criterion of >10% predicted. Those individuals had a high respiratory burden, suggesting the 2022 criterion would underdiagnose asthma, particularly in younger males. GINA 2026 retains the 2005 ERS/ATS criterion. Reference details: cited as references 18–19 in GINA 2026.
